I use multiple methods to study novel questions about the future of work. In my dissertation research, I show how young workers manage uncertainty and create meaning through participation in labor and financial markets. I also study how political polarization manifests as tension at work. Throughout, I consider what these changes mean for organizations—which must attract and retain workers whose attachments are shifting—and for work’s traditional role as an engine of mobility.

My mixed-methods work often uses quantitative data to establish trends in behavior and qualitative data from life history and in-depth interviews to uncover social psychological and organizational mechanisms. I am also skilled in computational text analysis.
