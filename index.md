I study the changing world of work, and what those changes mean for intergroup inequalities, organizations, and markets. In my dissertation research, I show how young workers manage uncertainty and create meaning through participation in labor and financial markets. I also study how political polarization manifests as tension at work. 

I use mixed methods, often pairing survey data to establish trends in behavior with interviews to uncover social psychological and organizational mechanisms. I am skilled in computational text analysis, which I use to study culture at scale.

My research has won awards from the University of California and the London School of Economics, and has been featured in the [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/personal-finance/gen-z-investments-home-ownership-ec0bbe98?st=1Z6MTA&reflink=desktopwebshare_permalink), [UC San Diego Today](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/gen-z-is-the-most-investment-savvy-generation-what), [The Last Show podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/ca/podcast/a-return-from-the-desert-september-8-2025/id1168275879?i=1000725673711), and [ASA's Economic Sociology section newsletter](https://www.asanet.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/Economic-Sociology-2023-Fall.pdf). Coverage of my work before graduate school leading start-up nonprofit organizations can be found in [the Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2018/may/02/offshore-secrecy-inside-the-movement-to-crack-it-open) and in [the Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/local/dc-council-holds-roundtable-on-street-harassment/2015/12/08/4e39135e-9d28-11e5-8728-1af6af208198_story.html).
